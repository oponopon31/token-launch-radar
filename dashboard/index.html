How implied value is computed

Every row is scored by one function of the snapshot figures. No protocol name is special-cased, and no row is hand-picked. The multiple is not a hand-set number: it is a size curve fitted to the 128 live-token protocols that record a market cap.

The blind test

Leave-one-out over the same 128 comparables: each held-out token is priced by a model fit without it. The board prices every row with the bare fitted size curve — row three — because the radar-5 full formula (curve plus bounded growth, maturity and breadth priors, row five) costs accuracy on every blind test: 1.224 against 1.326 leave-one-out, 1.266 against 1.296 in 5-fold, 1.129 against 1.146 pooled walk-forward. The priors are dropped, not hidden: their radar-5 weights are kept only in the validation comparison.

Lower median log error is better; within 2× is the share of held-out tokens priced within a factor of two.

Radar-5 priced rows with three bounded priors — growth (7-day TVL change, clamped to −80…300% and dampened with tanh), maturity (0.75 rising toward 1.10 over about 540 days with no token) and breadth (4% per extra chain, up to 5). Radar-6 drops all three: the bare curve beats the full radar-5 formula on leave-one-out, 5-fold and pooled walk-forward alike, so the board's implied value is now the bare curve alone, and the growth, maturity and breadth multipliers on every row are exactly 1. The fitted terms were already rejected in radar-4 — a fitted growth coefficient comes out negative and flips sign across folds — so fitting them would be fitting noise, and the model says so instead of hiding it. One consequence, stated plainly: implied value is now a strictly increasing function of TVL alone, so the board's ranking is the TVL ranking; the curve's work is the dollar value and the verdict, not an ordering the blind test could not justify.

How a farm plan is netted

Each plan totals its cash cost — gas for the first transaction, the setup, and one weekly action over the hold — and the hours it takes, then nets that against a stated scenario: the launch distributes 5% of its implied value to farmers, split equally among 5,000 active farmers. The plan shows the expected share, the net, the dollars per hour, and the break-even capture (the share of the implied launch value that pays the plan's cash cost, which needs no scenario at all). The verdict is FARM, THIN or SKIP from break-even odds: the launch value that exactly covers the plan's gas cost is the cash cost divided by the farmer's share of the launch (5% among 5,000), and the odds are the chance the launch clears that value under the model's error distribution (residual SD 2.077). FARM at 90% or better, THIN at 75% or better, SKIP below that. The odds condition on the token launching at all.

Read this honestly: implied value is a model, not a price. And “records no token” means the DefiLlama index records none for that protocol; it is not proof that no token exists.

Walk-forward validation — the curve tested on time, not shuffled

Leave-one-out and 5-fold splits shuffle the comparables, so a token listed last month can sit in the training set of a token listed three years ago. Walk-forward does not leak time: at each cutoff the curve is fit on the listings before it and tested on the listings after it. Only the 95 comparables with a listing date can be ordered in time; the 33 without one sit outside these splits, and the table says so. Read the numbers honestly: the test market cap is the snapshot-date market cap, so a split tests whether a curve fit on older listings predicts the current market cap of newer listings — not a launch-day prediction.

Lower median log error is better; within 2× is the share of held-out tokens priced within a factor of two. Pooled is all three test sets together: 101 predictions over 51 distinct tokens — a token listed after a cutoff is tested at every later cutoff too, so the newest listings count more than once.

Term-by-term ablation — what each term costs out of sample

Each row fits the named terms to log(market cap) alongside log(TVL) and scores the fit two ways: leave-one-out over all 128 comparables, and the pooled walk-forward test errors above. The bare curve wins leave-one-out, and every fitted term hurts it. Growth's small walk-forward pooled gain (1.102 vs 1.129) comes with a worse within-2× share and flips sign across the splits, so no fitted term is adopted — the board keeps the bare curve alone.

Verdict backtest — the rule tested on the comparables

Each comparable is priced by a curve fit without it, given the farm plan its own TVL and age would have earned, and checked against its actual market cap: did the launch value clear the break-even value, and what would the farm have netted? The "launch value" here is the snapshot-date market cap, not a launch-day value.

Shrunk category shifts — tested, not adopted

Category intercepts shrunk toward the global curve were tested at four strengths and hurt median log error at every one, in leave-one-out and on every walk-forward split — the category means are mostly noise next to a residual standard deviation above 2 in log space — so the board uses no category shift. One exception, on the secondary metric: leave-one-out within-2× at k=5 is 33.6% against 32.0% for the bare curve.
